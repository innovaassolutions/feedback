A course is a series of sessions: one for each class, workshop or module. Instructors set them up under **Course setup** in the course header. Its **Sessions** tab lists them in order, and shows for each what's done (✓) and what's left (○): its documents, its brief, and whether its feedback is open.

The **Learners** and **Interview guide** tabs, and **Instructors and sponsors**, apply to the whole course rather than to one session.

## Adding a session

At the end of the list, under **Add session**, enter a **Title**, the **Date** and the **Objectives**: what learners should be able to do afterwards. Choose **Add session**. It's numbered after the last one and starts as a draft, which learners can't see.

## Setting up a session

Open the session from the list. Its page takes you through three steps:

1. **Documents**: upload its slides, handouts and notes, and the transcript of its recording (as **Agents only**).
2. **Session brief**: the librarian drafts it from the documents; edit it and choose **Approve brief**.
3. **Feedback**: after the session, choose **Open feedback** and share the join link or QR code.

The links at the bottom go to the previous and next sessions. See [Documents and the session brief](/docs/running-a-course/documents-and-brief).

## Session states

| State | On screen | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | **Draft** | Being set up. Hidden from learners |
| Scheduled | **Upcoming** | Visible to learners, not delivered yet |
| Open | **Feedback open** | Delivered. Feedback and Q&A are open |
| Closed | **Closed** | The feedback window has ended. Q&A stays open |

A session moves forward one step at a time: **Show to learners** (Draft → Upcoming), **Open feedback** (Upcoming → Feedback open), then **Close feedback**. Each session says in a line what its state means. It can't move back. This keeps the record straight: feedback given on an open session can't end up on a session that was never delivered.

When you choose **Open feedback**, its feedback window starts. It closes by itself after a week (the course's feedback window), and you can close it sooner yourself.

## Reminders

Learners who haven't given feedback get two emails: one a day after the session opens, and one a day before it closes. Learners who have submitted get none.

## The join link and QR code

Each session's setup page shows its join link and a QR code under **Feedback**. Put the QR code on your last slide: learners scan it in the room and go straight to the feedback interview, signing in first if they need to. Feedback collected in the room gets far more responses than a link sent later.

## A good rhythm

1. Before the course: add all its sessions, so learners can see what's coming.
2. Before each session: upload its materials, approve its brief, and choose **Show to learners**.
3. At the end of the session: upload the recording's transcript, choose **Open feedback** and show the QR code.
4. Before the next session: read the results, record what you changed in **You said, we did**.
